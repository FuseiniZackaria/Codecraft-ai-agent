# Why CodeCraft

A plain-language case for a business owner thinking about whether to buy.

---

## The problem

Running a small or medium business — especially in Ghana and the rest of
Africa — comes with a familiar set of squeezes:

- **Too little time.** The owner is the salesperson, the marketer, the
  customer support rep, the strategist, and the one buying stationery. Every
  hour spent on inbox triage is an hour not spent on the business.
- **Can't afford a full team.** Hiring a sales rep + a marketer + a
  support person + a VA is tens of thousands of cedis a month in salary
  alone, before equipment and taxes. Most SMBs can't justify it.
- **Slow follow-ups.** A warm lead goes cold in 48 hours. Most owners
  can't follow up fast enough, and leads leak out the top of the funnel.
- **Missed leads.** WhatsApp messages pile up overnight. By the time the
  owner replies, the customer has already bought from a competitor.
- **Inbox overload.** Hundreds of emails a week; the three that actually
  need an answer are buried under newsletters, autoresponders, and old
  threads.
- **Inconsistent marketing.** Posts go up when the owner has time, which is
  never. The brand voice drifts. There's no editorial calendar.

---

## What CodeCraft is

CodeCraft is a team of digital assistants organised like a small company —
Sales, Marketing, Support, Strategy, Development — that drafts the day-to-day
busywork of running a business and asks the owner to approve anything that
actually goes out.

---

## Benefits by department

### Sales

- Drafts personalised outreach emails and WhatsApp messages at the owner's
  direction.
- Finds leads via web search.
- Verifies that a job listing or company is real before any outreach goes out.
- Tracks sent messages and queues Day 4 / 10 / 21 follow-ups automatically.
- Detects replies and stops follow-ups the moment a real response arrives.

**Result:** more consistent top-of-funnel activity, no stale follow-ups going
to someone who already said yes.

### Marketing

- Drafts social posts in the business's voice.
- Builds full content packages — caption, hashtags, cover image — for events,
  launches, or specials.

**Result:** regular, on-brand marketing without hiring an agency.

### Customer Support & Communication

- Triages the inbox and drafts replies to messages that genuinely need one.
- Drafts replies to incoming WhatsApp and Telegram messages the moment they
  arrive. Pings the owner about every one until the owner opts in to
  auto-reply.
- Books meetings on the owner's real calendar after proposing genuinely free
  slots.

**Result:** faster customer response times; no missed messages overnight;
no accidental double-booking.

### Strategy & Research

- A CEO-style thinking partner for weighing tradeoffs.
- Deep research with cited sources.
- Recurring daily or weekly briefings that highlight what's changed since the
  last run.

**Result:** better decisions without hours of browsing.

### Development

- Builds small coded artifacts — one-page flyers, landing pages, spreadsheet
  templates.
- Operates the owner's computer for find/organise/delete tasks.
- Navigates and summarises websites.

**Result:** removes small technical chores that would otherwise take a
freelancer or an unpaid evening.

## Why it's different

- **Works 24/7 and on a schedule.** Workflows run even while the owner sleeps.
  A daily briefing lands on WhatsApp at 7am. A customer message at 2am gets
  an acknowledgement at 2:00:30am.
- **Nothing goes out without approval.** Every send, every delete, every
  calendar event lands as a Tasks-page card. The owner clicks Approve or
  Reject.
- **Everything in one place.** No more juggling Gmail, WhatsApp Business,
  Instagram DMs, and a notes app. One dashboard.
- **Built for local realities.** WhatsApp is a first-class channel, not an
  afterthought. Phone contacts are treated the same as email contacts.
  Twilio Sandbox works out of the box for a demo; Meta Cloud API works when
  the business is approved.
- **Each business has its own private workspace.** No one else sees your
  data. The system actively guards against cross-workspace leaks and shows
  you a health panel confirming it.

## Example scenarios

> These are illustrative scenarios, not real customers.

### Example scenario: Kofi runs a WhatsApp-first catering business

Kofi gets 15–25 enquiries a day on WhatsApp, most asking the same four
questions (menu, price, delivery area, availability for a date). He loses
sleep because customers message at 10pm and he's not seeing them until the
morning.

With CodeCraft:

- Every incoming WhatsApp message gets an auto-drafted reply within seconds.
- Kofi gets a Telegram ping on his personal phone with the draft attached.
- He taps **Approve** on his way to bed; the reply lands with the customer at
  10:02pm.
- Over a week, response times drop from hours to seconds; conversion from
  enquiry to order goes up.

### Example scenario: Ama runs a boutique hotel in Cape Coast

Ama competes with bigger hotels on service, not price. Her biggest leak is
emails from international guests asking about availability — she replies in
~18 hours, which is enough for the guest to have booked a competitor.

With CodeCraft:

- The Personal Assistant agent triages the inbox each morning, drafts replies
  to the three enquiries that actually need one, and queues them.
- Ama reviews and approves in 10 minutes.
- Response time drops from 18 hours to ~2 hours; booking conversion
  improves.

### Example scenario: Yaw runs a construction supply shop

Yaw sells to small builders. His sales activity is sporadic — he does
outreach when he has time, which is rare. He has a list of 200 old customers
in a spreadsheet.

With CodeCraft:

- Yaw sets up a weekly workflow: "Draft a WhatsApp message to [customer]
  checking if they need anything for an upcoming job."
- The agent drafts 10 messages a week, personalising each from Yaw's notes.
- Yaw approves 7 of them in 5 minutes on his phone; the Response Detection
  agent stops follow-ups the moment a customer replies.
- Dormant customers come back without Yaw needing to carve out a sales
  afternoon.

### Example scenario: Akosua runs an event-services business

Akosua books 2–3 events a week. Each event needs a flyer, a WhatsApp
broadcast, and a landing page. She's been paying a freelance designer
GHS 150 per flyer.

With CodeCraft:

- Content Studio agent drafts a full content package per event: caption,
  hashtags, cover image.
- Coding agent builds a one-page landing page.
- Akosua reviews and publishes; freelancer bill drops to zero for
  single-day events.

## Who it's for

- A solo founder or small team doing all the roles themselves.
- A business that gets most of its enquiries on WhatsApp, Instagram, or email.
- A business that needs follow-through, not just ideas.
- Someone comfortable approving drafts rather than letting an AI loose.

## Who it's NOT for

- A business that wants fully autonomous sending with no human in the loop
  (the approval gate is deliberate and not optional).
- A regulated industry where every outbound message needs compliance review
  (CodeCraft doesn't do that review for you).
- A business whose customers only communicate in-person or by phone.
- An enterprise with a dedicated sales ops / marketing ops team already —
  the agents will feel redundant.

## Common questions

**Q: Will it send messages without me?**
A: No. Every email, every WhatsApp, every calendar invite is drafted and
queued. You click Approve on the Tasks page before anything leaves the
system. Even optional auto-reply modes refuse to send anything that mentions
refunds, complaints, or placeholder text, and are rate-limited per day.

**Q: Is my data private?**
A: Yes. Each business has its own private workspace in the database. The
system has an active guard that logs or blocks any attempt to read or write
rows outside the correct workspace, and ships with a health panel so you can
see for yourself that nothing's leaking.

**Q: Do I need technical skills?**
A: No. The owner never writes code. Someone with basic admin skills does the
one-time setup (connecting Gmail, WhatsApp, etc.); after that you use a chat
window and a Tasks page.

**Q: What if it makes a mistake?**
A: You see every outbound message before it goes. If the Research or Briefing
agent gets a fact wrong, you catch it when you read the output. If an agent
is confused and asks a clarifying question, answer it in chat. If an agent
is clearly wrong, reject the draft on the Tasks page.

**Q: What does it cost to run?**
A: The CodeCraft subscription itself: `[PRICE - to be set]`. On top of that
you pay the usual costs of the services you connect — your Gmail, your
Twilio or Meta WhatsApp, the LLM provider (Anthropic/OpenAI). There's a hard
daily spend cap on Ian's chat (`ASSISTANT_DAILY_USD_CAP`), so you can't run
up a surprise bill overnight.

**Q: What happens if the system crashes?**
A: Scheduled workflows pick up again when the server comes back; pending
approvals persist in the database. No work is lost. (On the free Render
plan, the backend sleeps after 15 minutes of inactivity — scheduled
workflows pause while asleep. Upgrade to a paid tier for reliable scheduling.)
