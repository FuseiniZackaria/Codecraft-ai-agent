SEC. 01 — METHOD MATERIAL: PRACTICE QTY: WEEKLY

The shop floor, not the classroom.

Three things happen every week, on repeat, until they're muscle memory.

✎

Assigned stock

A real problem, cut to your track's grain — not a toy kata. You work it alone first, tools down before the bell.

▤

Bench sessions

Live, camera-on pairing with a senior engineer watching your hands on the keyboard, not just your finished PR.

✓

Senior review

Line-by-line marks on the drawing: what's load-bearing, what's decorative, what won't survive contact with production.

SEC. 02 — APPRENTICESHIP TRACKS SEQUENCE: LINEAR DURATION: 4–6 MO EACH

Three stations. One bench.

You move through them in order — each one depends on the joints made in the last.

01

Foundations

Hand tools

Syntax as muscle memory, data structures by feel, git without panic, debugging as a calm, repeatable procedure. You leave able to read any codebase without flinching.

  • Reading code
  • Data structures
  • Debugging
  • Version control
02

Joinery

How things connect

APIs, databases, queues, state — the joints that hold a system together under load. You practice making connections that don't creak two years from now.

  • APIs & services
  • Data modeling
  • Concurrency
  • System design
03

Finishing

Sand, polish, ship

Code review as craft, refactoring without fear, production readiness, and the taste to know when a thing is actually done — not just working.

  • Code review
  • Refactoring
  • Observability
  • Production taste
SEC. 03 — LIVE BENCH SESSIONS FORMAT: SYNCHRONOUS CAP: 6 PER BENCH

Standing appointments at the bench.

Small groups, cameras on, one senior engineer per bench. You bring the problem you're stuck on — real ones only.

TUESDAY

The Bench

Pairing block. You drive, a senior engineer narrates the shop-floor thinking you can't get from a diff: why this abstraction, why not that one yet.

THURSDAY

Teardown

A production incident or gnarly PR, opened up on the table and taken apart in front of the group, joint by joint.

MONTHLY

Crit Night

You present a piece of your own work for open review — brass-tacks feedback, delivered the way a foreman marks up a drawing.
